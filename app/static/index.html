Plain-language guide

Sign
The sky is divided into 12 equal parts (Aries, Taurus …). "Jupiter in Taurus" means Jupiter is in that part of the sky.
Nakshatra
The Vedic system also divides the sky into 27 smaller parts called nakshatras (Ashwini, Rohini …). The Moon passes through one roughly every day.
Retrograde
A planet seen from Earth sometimes appears to move backwards for a few weeks. Mercury does this about three times a year.
Together (conjunction)
Two planets in nearly the same spot in the sky.
60° apart (sextile) · 120° apart (trine)
Angles traditionally read as easy or harmonious.
90° apart (square) · opposite (180°)
Angles traditionally read as tense or challenging.
Rahu and Ketu
The two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's path; eclipses happen near them. They are always exactly opposite each other.
Tithi and paksha
The lunar day (1–30). Shukla paksha is the waxing (growing) Moon, Krishna paksha the waning Moon.
Vedic vs Western
The two systems place the signs about 24° apart. Vedic follows the stars (Lahiri), Western follows the seasons.
Learning years, checking years, final test
The history is split in three. Patterns are searched for only in the oldest part. A pattern counts only if it keeps working in the newer years it has never seen, the way a real effect would.
Luck check
We run the same search on the same prices, deliberately shifted in time so the planets cannot possibly match them. Whatever that search "finds" is pure luck. Real patterns must clearly beat it.
Why so strict?
When you test tens of thousands of combinations, some will look impressive by pure chance, the way someone among thousands of coin-tossers will flip ten heads in a row. The checks separate real patterns from those lucky streaks.
